A short, interactive course

Sensitivity Analysis for Causal Inference

Read, compute, and critique the sensitivity analyses that appear in clinical real-world-data and causal-ML studies — so you can tell when "robust to unmeasured confounding" is earned, overstated, or missing.

The mission. Fluency, not implementation. Given any RWD paper, interpret its E-value, robustness value, Γ, bounds, or negative control; name the family it belongs to and when that family applies; and spot the standard misuses. Each lesson is one tangible win — tap, read, do the interactive check, then ask your teacher follow-ups.

Lessons

L01What a Sensitivity Analysis Actually Asks

The reframe behind every method: not "is there confounding?" but "how strong would it have to be?" Plus the E-value — the number you'll see most — with a live calculator.

Skill · read, compute & critique an E-value
L02Two Postures: Worst-Case Bounds vs Parametric

The top-level map. Assumption-free bounds (Manski) vs calibrated "how-much-confounding" methods — and where the E-value sits on the spectrum.

Skill · place any method on the spectrum
L03Rosenbaum Γ-Bounds for Matched Designs

The design-based calibration for matched studies. Read a Γ, judge robustness — and avoid the trap of treating Γ (a treatment-leg dial) as the whole confounder strength.

Skill · interpret a Γ robustness statement
L04The Robustness Value & Contour Plots

The core mission skill: read an OVB contour plot and a robustness value, benchmark against measured covariates — for linear models and causal ML (sensemakr / dml.sensemakr) alike.

Skill · read an RV + contour plot (interactive plot)
L05Negative Controls: the Design-Based Detector

The last family. A tripwire for "your pipeline is biased" — its two make-or-break assumptions, and why it detects confounding rather than quantifying it.

Skill · judge & interpret negative controls
